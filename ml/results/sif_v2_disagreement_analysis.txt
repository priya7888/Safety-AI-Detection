2. ROOT CAUSE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Cause: Rule-based keyword matching rigidity (3 of 4 cases: 4, 7, 9).
  The rule engine depends on exact lexicon dictionary matches to populate
  the `hazard` variable. When phrasing varies naturally (e.g. "power cable"
  instead of "arc flash", or "fell from Derrick" instead of "dropped object"),
  the rule engine either drops to INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION or defaults to NO.
- Secondary Cause: Rule fallback conservatism (2 of 4 cases: 9, 10).
  The rule engine conservatively withholds a "NO" unless an explicit low-energy
  hazard label is detected, while the ML model confidently assigns Non-SIF.
- Tertiary Cause: Statistical boundary uncertainty (1 case: Case 4).
  Environmental co-occurrences in synthetic training text caused ML confidence
  to sit at 54.50%.
- Suspicious Cases: 0. None of the disagreements indicate data corruption,
  leakage exploits, or catastrophic model failure.
